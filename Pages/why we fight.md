# We take our differences seriously

Wars continue to devastate lives across the globe. Conflicts are not new: every species has faced challenges of survival. Surviving in harsh environments, whether the ice-cold tundra or the scorching Sahara, has shaped instincts that include competition and conflict.

Even the origins of individuals involve contest: hundreds of millions of sperm cells compete, yet only one fertilizes the egg. The odds that you are here are tiny, and yet here we are.

Why do humans, as a species, keep returning to war despite its costs?

Looking at our history, fighting over limited resources often led to one group gaining control while others lost access–an imbalance that echoes in feudal systems and modern inequalities. Divisions arise from many causes, and sometimes we emphasize difference even when we are similar: the "Us vs. Them" feeling, as Pink Floyd explores in "Us and Them".

This difference-making mindset has evolutionary and cultural roots. How do we move beyond it? Technology and information access alone won't solve these issues, though they can help when combined with positive social change. Communication platforms can amplify both signal and noise; distorted news and polarising content can reinforce division.

Being a good listener means holding difficult ideas and wrestling with them patiently. It takes effort and bandwidth to analyze opposing arguments rather than reacting to the first impression.

Can violence lead to lasting peace? No—resolving conflicts requires addressing internal and structural causes, not just force.

We should aim to use technology for good, and to strengthen civic norms that support cooperation and understanding.
